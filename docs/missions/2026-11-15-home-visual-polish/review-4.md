On zero-based leg 3 (toward t=1), the final first input frame changes pitch 3.624°→3.821° and height 7.033→7.021 m; all following samples settle monotonically. On leg 7 (toward t=0), the first frame changes pitch 6.123°→6.034°, then all following samples rise monotonically. Removing only the pre-burst sample makes both reported backtracking totals zero. The base has the same first-frame changes and the same summary maxima (0.1968° pitch, 0.0113 m height). `settle.py` dispatches the complete touch drag synchronously between these samples; the app also applies the finger's final position to camera parallax. Thus these are real camera offsets induced by the synthetic input, classified as rebound by the metric; they are not a floating-point error or a remaining oscillation of the climb curve. They do not prove real-device gesture quality. The report's broad “no leg overshoots” is consistent with zero overshoot, but must not be read as zero backtracking on every raw leg.

### Ear solver, exclusions and cost

The per-frame solve runs after updating the head pose. A probe is mirrored/rotated in the hinge frame, transformed through the head and then divided by the barrel's ellipsoid scale. Shared body transforms cancel correctly in that relative frame. A coarse lift search followed by binary refinement finds a clear pose; the fan spring is limited against the current constraint, and lift is kept at least as high as the computed requirement. `2a6ed4c` computes its quaternion once per candidate and reuses the solved lift when the actual fan equals the target, preserving the geometry test while removing redundant work.

Independent diagnostic: three 120 s simulations, 7,500 frames each at 16 ms, seeded wander, three viewport compositions (1440×900, 390×844, 844×390), camera travelling the full climb and back, repeated pokes, and every one of the 2,790 ear vertices measured per frame against head/barrel volumes. Result: zero frames with blade in body, zero with blade folded into head, zero hidden-ear observations. Least clearance was ≈3.60–3.69 cm and largest lift ≈1.197 rad. Current pose time averaged ≈0.058–0.060 ms on this Node run. This is a CPU diagnostic with a stub canvas for the shadow texture, not a rendered FPS test or a reproduction of the executor's seed/obstacles. Its clearance is lower than the recorded ≈4.1 cm because it exercises a different motion sample; the report's 4.1 cm is accurate for its stated run, not a universal lower bound.

The root exclusions are reasonable for joined geometry, with qualifications. The runtime solver samples every fifth blade vertex from local x≥0.16. The evidence checker tests every vertex, but permits head-hidden vertices through x=0.3 and separately counts non-blade roots entering the shoulder. The hinge lies inside the head: requiring zero intersection of that root would contradict an attached ear. Inspected comparison/extreme boards support the attachment and blade separation. These exclusions do not establish that every excluded root vertex is always invisible: the checker permits some outside the head at the head/shoulder junction (up to 281 in the recorded run, 294 in my different sample). They therefore need the accompanying visual checks, not a claim of mathematically zero ear/body intersection everywhere.

No collision guarantee is established for triangle interiors, hat, trunk, legs, every random future pose or every dt. The search also has a maximum-lift fallback rather than a final all-vertex assertion. No failing supported pose was found. Camera angle does not change geometric clearance, but it can expose a seam; the recorded minute-long ear checks do not themselves orbit through every climb bearing. Static turntable/extreme samples and my orbiting numerical run complement, rather than replace, playback.

The source optimization is supported and the current cost is independently measured. A separate attempt to time the old `725a942` implementation failed with `Error: spawnSync git EPERM`; I did not widen permissions. Consequently the old/new 0.14→0.05 ms benchmark remains executor evidence. The raw recorded FPS intervals independently confirm that the final performance margin still needs resolution (F8).
