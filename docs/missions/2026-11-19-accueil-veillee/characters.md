## Ambiguities raised to the owner (via pilotage)

- A1 `Kompè` vs `Konpè`: only `Konpè` (n before p) is attested in written Guadeloupean Creole (S8, S3, S4). `Kompè` not found. Provisional: `Konpè`.
- A2 `Komè` (commère, female) vs `Konpè`: Zamba is male in every source (« Compère Zamba », courts Mlle Sapotille in S1). No « Commère/Komè Zamba » found; the only commère of the corpus found is « Ma-Commère Tortue » (S3). Provisional: `Konpè Zanba`, male. If the owner wants a female character, it is a deliberate invention to state on the page, not the traditional Zamba.
- A3 `Zamba` vs `Zanba`: `Zanba` is the GEREC spelling (S8, nasal vowel); `Zamba` is the French-language spelling of the 1935 collection and of the 2012 Jasor book title (S1, S2). Provisional: `Zanba` in Kréyòl, `Zamba` in the French gloss.
- A4 Social reading: Zamba is often written as « sot / stupide » (S1, S3); Ina Césaire (S4, second-hand) warns against reading him as the oppressor, Benzo (S7, second-hand) as the cane-field slave. The page avoids « bête / stupide »: big, strong, always hungry, believes what he is told.

## Traits and gestures

- Konpè Lapen: tiny, clever, inventive, a trickster who fools the strong to survive (S1 « petit et chétif, avait l'esprit inventif », S8 « Konpè Lapen toupiti. I wè. I ka kouyonné toutmoun. »). Concrete action: he pretends to have a fever so that Zamba carries him like a horse (S1, « Lapin fait sa monture de Zamba »). Gesture: one ear pivots towards a sound, then his eyes follow (owner's example). Reduced motion: ear already turned, eyes sideways, a sly smile.
- Konpè Zanba: a very big, very strong elephant, gluttonous (« glouton et vorace », S1), naive, always tricked by Lapin and ends « furieux et battu » (S1 preface). Concrete action: always hungry, his trunk smells out the smallest fruit. Gesture (chosen after checking the portrait: elephant, glutton): his trunk rises and curls towards a mango, sniffs twice, his ear flaps. Reduced motion: trunk raised towards the mango.

## Page texts (French, child level; names in Kréyòl)

Lapen: « Konpè Lapen est un tout petit lapin, mais sa tête est pleine d'idées. Un jour, il a fait semblant d'avoir la fièvre pour que Zamba le porte sur son dos comme un cheval ! Avec ses longues oreilles, rien ne lui échappe. »

Zanba: « Konpè Zanba est un éléphant immense et très fort. Il a toujours faim : sa trompe flaire le moindre fruit mûr. Il croit tout ce qu'on lui raconte, alors Konpè Lapen lui joue souvent des tours. »

No Kréyòl sentence is written beyond the names (Kréyòl versions of these texts need a Creole speaker: `docs/kreyol/AGENT_RULES.md`).
